## [Unreleased] - 2026-08-22

### Fixed
- **First-use flicker and lag** — every sprite was created and decoded the first time it was drawn, and an image that has not loaded draws nothing: the character blinked blank through its first walk/jump/attack cycle after login or an equip change, a skill's first cast strobed through its effect and stalled on the 2.7MB skill sound bank. Characters now warm every stance's parts in idle time after loading or changing gear, bound skills warm their art and clips when placed on the hotkey bar, and the sound bank loads with the map.
- **Drowning and cold** — Aqua Road takes 6 HP and El Nath 10 HP every 10 seconds (the maps' `decHP`), stopped by the Oxygen Tank / Cape of Warmness the maps name, or by an Air Bubble, Cassandra's Magic (underwater) or Soft White Bun (cold) — those timed consumables are now real buffs on the buff bar, and so are every other potion's timed stats (Ciders, Bubble Gum), which were silently dropped before.
- **Inventory window scrolls** — the grid showed the first 24 slots only and the VScr thumb was painted in place ("no scroll functionality yet"), so an ETC tab past 24 items was unreachable. Wheel over the window, the arrows, or a click on the track scroll a row at a time; the thumb tracks the position; tab switches reset it.
- **Swimming** — holding jump played the jump sound every frame (and re-kicked you up the moment you down-jumped through a submerged platform, so you could never drop through); a mob hit under water launched you like a jump. Now a press is one kick and a held key does nothing more (GMS tap-to-rise swimming), down+jump drops through, and an underwater hit is a sideways push only.
- **Shadow Partner** (Hermit / Night Walker) — was not implemented at all; now every basic attack, melee skill and thrown star while it lasts is followed by the shadow's copy of the same lines (`x`% basic / `y`% skill per the tooltip, Lucky Seven 2→4, Triple Throw 3→6), a dark translucent replay of your own pose trails you, and the cast burns a Summoning Rock like the tooltip says — buffs with an `itemCon` are now gated on both cast paths.
- **Explosion and Big Bang could not be cast as attacks** (they carry `mad`, no `damage`, and no hit/ball art); **Soul Arrow was cast as an attack** (its `ball` is the free arrow). Big Bang's hold-to-charge is not modelled — it fires once.
- **Multi-hit timing reviewed against the WZ** — yesterday's rule paced the later lines by whatever body frame came next and repeated the last frame's delay past the end, which was only right for stances authored with one attack frame per hit (Crusher, Brandish, Barrage, Savage Blow). Demolition's eight punches landed 2.7s apart (19s per cast), Vampire's four and Double Stab's second landed 450ms apart *after* the animation, Wind Shot's three were a single line, and a mob killed by the first line swallowed the rest of its column (one number on a Crusher kill). Now: a line per non-negative-delay body frame (Crusher's own effect art staggers 600/750/900 to prove it), then the skill's Hit-art cadence once the frames run out; later lines still print on a dying mob; knockback once per attack; projectile skills land `attackCount` lines per impact; the host draws a non-host's stacked column. **Assassinate** was classed as a buff (no hit art, a stun `time`) and could not attack at all — it is an attack with GMS's four strikes, the last lethal at `criticalDamage`% with `prop`% odds.
- **Multi-hit skills land hit by hit** — Spear Crusher, Brandish, Dragon Fury and every other `attackCount` skill applied all their lines in the same instant, so the numbers printed on top of each other and one thud played. Now the i-th line lands when the i-th body frame of the attack starts (the original client's timing), each with the mob's Damage sound and the skill's Hit clip, and the numbers stack up the column over the mob (24px a row, 36 after a critical).
- **The "GPU crash" was a render-loop exception** — a damage value that was `NaN` or fractional reached the WZ digit sprites (`DamageIndicator.drawDamage`, the status bar's `drawNumbers`), which only ship 0-9; the throw aborted the rest of every frame and left the last good frame smeared across the screen. HP/MP/EXP are now floored at the source (`hp`/`mp` setters, `addExp`, `takeDamage`), both renderers skip glyphs they don't have, and a non-finite value logs a stack trace instead of propagating.
- **Master Sergeant Fox's `#m211000000#`** — Say `#L` selection labels go through `resolveItemCodes()` like every other display site.
- **21 invisible mobs** (Werewolf, Tauromacis, the Golems, King Clang, Rombot, Tae Roon, King Sage Cat...) — full-size all-transparent PNGs from the original conversion, repaired with `tools/fix-blank-sprites.py --deep`.

### Removed
- **Private-server content** — the Maple Administrator's Maple-gear shop (El Nath, shop 9010000) and the two custom NPCs in the Free Market entrance. The Administrator now says her GMS `d0`/`d1` lines.

## [Unreleased] - 2026-08-18

### Added
- **Monster Book** — the whole v83 book, drawn from `UI.wz/UIWindow.img/MonsterBook`. All 343 cards come out of `Item.wz/Consume/0238.img`, where `info/mob` is the authority on which monster a card describes and every entry carries `info/monsterBook=1`; it matches the emulator's `monstercarddata` one for one, so nothing had to be taken on trust from outside the WZ. The nine bookmarks down the left edge are the monster's level band, keyed off the card id block and checked against Mob.wz — every 2380xxx card points at a level 1-15 monster, 2387xxx at 106+, and 2388xxx is SPECIAL (bosses and event monsters), which is also the split the book counts as "SPC CARD" against "REG CARD". Card ids are not contiguous inside a block, so a card's place in the 5x5 grid is its sorted index within its tab, never `id % 25`.
- **Cards are collected, not carried.** Their WZ spec has `consumeOnPickup`, so a card is spent the instant you touch it and never occupies an inventory slot. That is enforced at the pickup path and again in `Inventory.addToInventory`, the funnel every other grant runs through (quest and NPC script rewards included), so no route can drop one into a tab. Cards stranded in the USE tab by saves that predate the book are drained into it on login, alongside the existing cash-item expiry sweep.
- **A card reveals itself a copy at a time**, which is what `RightTab`'s `disabled` frame is for: one copy is the picture, name and level, two adds HP/MP, three the rest of the stat block and the EPISODE text, four the DROPPING list, five the FOUND IN list and the `fullMark` medal on the card. Book level follows v83's curve — 11 cards for level 2, then 31, 61, 101, 151, 211, 281 — and only the first copy of a card counts toward it. 343 cards exist, so 8 is the ceiling, which is exactly how many book covers `MonsterBook/icon` ships. Right-click a card you own to register it as your book cover.
- Per-monster stats, habitat and episode text are precomputed into `/data/monsterbook.json` by `tools/build-monsterbook-data.js`. Mob.wz averages 620KB an entry, peaks at 31MB for one boss and is evicted on every map change, so the book reads none of it at runtime; monster, map and item names still resolve through the usual String.wz tables. The card's own art stands in for the monster portrait for the same reason.
- The book persists as `characters.monsterbook` plus `monsterbook_cover`, and only a five-figure summary (level, cover, total, basic, special) reaches other players — enough to fill in the Monster Book section of their character info window, which had been showing placeholder zeroes. Opens with **B** by default; the action is v83's own (`MONSTER BOOK` is icon 22 of the KeyConfig strip) but Nexon shipped it unbound, so the key is ours and rebinds like any other.

### Fixed
- **Two-handed weapons were invisible in character select.** Spears, polearms, two-handed swords, axes and blunts, and crossbows are authored for `stand2`/`walk2` and ship no `stand1` node at all — the body's `stand2` is a different pose that adds the `hand` part the weapon anchors to. The preview was hardcoded to `stand1`, so the weapon layer found no frame and drew the character empty-handed, with no error anywhere: a missing stance yields an empty node and simply contributes nothing. The preview now takes its pose from the weapon's `info/stand` and `info/walk`, the way the in-game character already did. 322 weapons were affected, and the walk cycle mattered too — clicking a character switched it to `walk1`, which a spear also lacks.
- **Tall NPCs blinked out of view.** The viewport test measured an NPC's anchor point, but `cy` is its feet, so a tall sprite lives entirely above its own anchor: Perion's MapleTV (9250045) is 411x520 with origin (213,520). Climb above it and the anchor cleared the margin while 520px of billboard still covered the screen — the TV vanished when you stood on top of it. The test now uses the sprite's box. 32 NPCs have stand sprites over 300px tall and all of them flickered this way.
- **Happyville's chimney portal did nothing at all.** `pi.openNpc()` was a stub, so `in_xmas_party` opened nothing and returned false, which also blocks entry — the portal was silently inert in both directions. The NPC script behind it needs to measure your distance to the `chimney01` portal to choose its line, and both `getPosition()` and `getMap().getPortal()` were absent, degrading through the safe-stub proxy to NaN so the comparison was always false. With all three in place Santa now tells you off for sneaking down someone's chimney, which is the correct v83 behaviour — the portal is meant to refuse you, not warp you.
- **Marker NPCs showed a blank portrait.** The dialog already knew to fall back to `info/default` when an NPC's `stand/0` is a transparent placeholder, but the test only caught sprites 2px or narrower (written for Athena Pierce's 1x229 sliver on the ark) and only looked at width. Across all 6928 NPCs a `stand/0` is either 1px or 4px in its smaller dimension, and the smallest genuine sprite is 17 — so 4x4 markers like Santa (9209100, an invisible trigger with `hideName=1`) sailed through and drew nothing. 18 NPCs get their real portrait back.
- **Leaving Happyville dropped you in Ellinia instead of where you came from.** Rooney saves your origin map on the way in and the exit NPC reads it back, falling back to Ellinia when nothing is stored. `MapleMap.load` takes `number | string` (the login map is `"MapLogin"`) and set `this.mapId = id as number` — a compile-time cast that converts nothing, so an id that arrived as a string stayed one. The saved-location store gates on `typeof v === 'number'`, read a stored `"102000000"` as nothing saved, and the exit took its fallback. `mapId` is now genuinely numeric and both script engines coerce on the way in and out, which also fixes the strict `mapRef.mapId === def.map?.mapId` comparisons in the map-object code that would have silently failed the same way.
- **Review pass on the Monster Book** — the search field is a real DOM `<input>`, and quitting to login left it floating over the login screen with another added each time you re-entered; `quitToLogin` now closes the menus the way it already closed the chat box. Because that input sits above the canvas it also swallowed presses in its own rect no matter which window was on top, so an inventory slot dragged over it became a dead spot — it hides whenever another window is in front. Reopening the book now returns to the card grid rather than the summary page (and no longer clears the "new card" blink for a tab you never looked at), the context menu dismisses on a click outside, hovering a card no longer draws the blue frame that means "registered cover", and the book summary is change-gated on `player_update` like the pet roster instead of shipping 30 times a second.
- **The Cash Shop try-on went empty-handed with a two-handed weapon.** The preview's movement sim recomputed `stand1`/`walk1` every tick and pushed it back into the same character, overwriting the pose the weapon had just asked for — so the try-on lost the weapon the moment you moved. It reads the same `info/stand` / `info/walk` split now.
- `GUIUtil.imageInView` was passing `dx + img.width`, an absolute coordinate, into a `width` field that the overlap test adds to `x` again, so it reported hits far outside the image. Nothing calls it yet; it is the obvious helper to reach for the next time something needs culling.

## [Unreleased] - 2026-08-07

### Added
- **Aran and Cygnus Knights are creatable** — picking either on the race-select screen used to raise "an error occurred" and go nowhere; both now run the full create flow. Each race gets its own wardrobe out of `Etc.wz/MakeCharInfo.img`, which is where v83 keeps the three option sets: `Info` for Explorers, `PremiumChar*` for Cygnus (byte-identical to Info — a new Knight really does wear the same beginner clothes) and `OrientChar*` for Aran, whose faces are its own (20100/20401/20402 male, 21700/21201/21002 female) and whose outfit is fixed at one Simple Warrior Top, Simple Warrior Pants, Average Musashi Shoes and a Basic Polearm, so those rows have nothing to cycle. The gender row now swaps the whole set rather than just faces and hair — female Explorers had been wearing the male tops — and gender is finally *saved*, having been hardcoded to 0 on the way to the server. Characters are born into the right job and place: Beginner on Maple Island, Noblesse in Ereve (130030000), Legend in the Rien snowfield (914000000)
- **Per-job starting stats, owned by code** — v83 has no character-creation dice (Nexon dropped it around v56-v63), so every level 1 of a class starts identical: Explorers 12/5/4/4 — STR-weighted so a fresh Beginner can actually kill Snails — and Noblesse 6/6/6/6. `Character.create` now passes stats, HP/MP, level and mesos explicitly. They had been SQLite column *defaults*, which meant the code couldn't set them and a long-lived database silently outranked `db.js`: the live schema said 12/5 and 20,000 mesos while the committed file said 4/4 and 0, and `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` never reconciles an existing table. Creation also refuses any job outside {0, 1000, 2000} — it's the one place a client could otherwise hand itself a job it never advanced into. The dead dice code in `UILogin` (commented-out roll button, `newCharStats` computed and never read) is gone, with a note recording why `Login.img` still ships `dice` and `statTb` art so nobody re-adds the feature from the leftovers. Aran's authentic spread isn't documented anywhere I could find, online or in the WZ — it uses the Explorer one as a flagged placeholder
- **W opens the world map, and it's rebindable** — the world map had no key at all, only the minimap's WORLD button. It's now a first-class keyboard action bound to **W** by default, so it appears in KEYBOARD SETTING like every other control: draggable off its key, droppable onto another, and sitting in the palette if you unbind it. Its icon is the WORLD MAP sprite v83 already ships at `KeyConfig/icon/5` (between BUDDY and MESSENGER), read off the artwork rather than guessed. Characters saved before this existed pick it up on W automatically — through both the localStorage and the per-character DB restore paths — but only when W is still free, so an existing rebind is never displaced. Pressing W again closes it, since the menu toggles sit outside the `dialogOpen` input gate
- **The minimap tells you which NPCs have quests** — every NPC was a plain green dot, but v83 ships three markers under `Map.wz/MapHelper.img/minimap` and we only ever loaded one: `npc` (4×7 dot), `startnpc` (4×13, dot plus a yellow **!**) where a quest can be picked up, and `endnpc` (4×13, dot plus a yellow **?**) where one can be handed in. The taller two are anchored by their base so the glyph rises above the dot rather than shifting it off the NPC's spot. NPC dots moved out of the cached static frame into the per-frame pass, since the marker changes the moment a quest is accepted or turned in. The state itself is derived once per NPC every 250ms in `NPC.update` — not per draw, and not gated on being on screen, which the minimap needs — and the overhead notice now reads that same value, so the two can't disagree
- **NPCs honor their authored click box** — `info/dcLeft/dcTop/dcRight/dcBottom` is v83's real hit rectangle and 5,893 of ~6,900 NPCs declare one; we had been using the sprite frame alone. For most NPCs the box just restates the sprite, so the two are unioned and nothing that was clickable stops being clickable. It matters for the NPCs painted into map scenery, which exist only as an invisible hitbox somewhere else entirely: Athena Pierce on the ark (1209007) is a 1×229 fully transparent strip at the foot of a ladder whose box sits 150-210px above, over the artwork — a one-pixel-wide target. The quest notice now hovers a fixed 12px above the NPC's own head rather than a hardcoded distance above its feet, which had buried it inside tall sprites and stranded it at the bottom of Athena's ladder while she leaned out of a window 200px up. It measures from the *sprite*, not the click box — a click box is a generous target and is routinely far taller than the art (1052116 declares `dcTop -135` over a 68px sprite) — falling back to the box only for the scenery-painted NPCs whose sprite is a transparent sliver

### Fixed
- **Attack reach came from an invented constant instead of the WZ** — magic bolts reached 400px, half the viewport, which reads as no limit at all. v83 states reach three ways and which one a skill uses is meaningful: an explicit `range` (25 attacking skills, 100-600, most commonly 130), an `lt`/`rb` rectangle centred on the character (88 skills, and only ever on AREA skills — Thunder Bolt is 300x100 next to mobCount 6, Heal 500x300), or nothing at all (107 skills — every single-target attack in the game, Energy Bolt, Magic Claw and Power Strike among them, where the original client falls back to the character's own reach rather than reading data). `lt`/`rb` was not parsed at all, so Thunder Bolt's authored box was being replaced by the same flat 400 as everything else. `skillReach()` now resolves in that order — `range`, then the rectangle's half-width, then a single named `SINGLE_TARGET_REACH`. That last constant is the one number v83 does not supply and is marked as ours: 200px, inside the 100-200 band Nexon uses for close-range skills and about 3x a wand's 70px swing. Basic ranged attacks (bow, crossbow, claw) fall in the same category — single-target, no skill geometry — and had a separate hardcoded 600, plus two more stale defaults inside `Projectile` itself (400 and 1000). All four are gone; there is now exactly one reach constant in the client
- **Casting a magic bolt could crash the game loop every frame** — `Cannot read properties of null (reading 'centerPosition')` out of `Projectile.findTargetForSkill`, thrown on every attack once it started. Target selection filtered monsters by range and then looped to pick the nearest, assuming a non-empty candidate list guaranteed a winner. It doesn't: `Monster.centerPosition` is null until `Monster.load()` fills it, and both the range test and the distance test compare with `>` and `<`, so a mob that is still loading (or whose position has gone non-finite) fails *every* comparison — NaN compares false either way — slipping through the filter and then never winning the loop. `closestMonster` stayed null and was dereferenced unguarded. Both copies of the pattern now share one `pickNearestTarget()` that drops monsters without a finite centre up front and returns null honestly, which the callers check
- **One-shot animations strobed through their first play** — level-up, quest-clear, quest-start, job-advancement, EXP-gain and the MapleTV ad all flickered. `nGetImage()` builds a frame's image lazily and `drawImage` skips one that hasn't finished decoding, so an effect that reaches most of its frames for the very first time *while playing* draws nothing on each new frame until its decode lands — and the level-up frames grow to 296×329, which is long enough to see. NPC stances already worked around this by decoding up front; that workaround is now a shared `preloadFrames()` helper on `WZNode`, awaited before an effect activates (guaranteeing a clean first run) and fired-and-forgotten for looping sprites like the TV ad and everything built on `FrameAnimation`, which covers the login screen's animations too
